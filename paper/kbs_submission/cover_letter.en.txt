Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “VulTriage: Proof-Carrying Detector--Shift Knowledge Contracts for Selective Vulnerability Triage,” for publication in Knowledge-Based Systems.

The manuscript addresses a knowledge-based decision-support problem that detector ranking metrics do not capture: what evidence and authority must exist before a cross-project vulnerability detector may release an automatic label? VulTriage represents that authority as an executable, proof-carrying detector--shift contract. A qualification registry binds authority to a detector, source domain, target domain, and policy version. Three-valued knowledge rules evaluate budget validity, evidence completeness, gate support, and singleton eligibility; false, unknown, mismatched, or unregistered states fail closed to review. Each automatic action carries a machine-verifiable certificate that binds the identity, budget, prediction set, rule truth values, and evidence digest.

The frozen, prediction-sealed study uses PrimeVul for retrospective controller development and 24 external DiverseVul projects for evaluation. On a fixed hash-defined 12/12 registration--confirmation split, hashing-SGD registers at qualification score Q=0.757 and confirms at Q=0.917. It qualifies on both halves in 84.94% of 10,000 deterministic project partitions; on the fixed confirmation half, all six non-severe projects are eligible and five of six severe projects are review-only. The same firewall withholds CodeBERT authority at registration (Q=0.150), thereby turning unsupported detector transfer into an executable review-only decision. Independently, project-aware weighting improves hashing-SGD risk alignment on all 24 projects at the primary operating point and on 202 of 216 frozen project--budget cells. The direction persists across calibration-size and near-duplicate sensitivity analyses.

Relative to recent policy-governed vulnerability management, the manuscript contributes an identity-bound authorization mechanism rather than another detector or policy threshold: detector--shift qualification is registered before item-level execution, missing evidence is represented in three-valued logic, and automatic actions carry certificates that are verified by complete contract re-execution. This combination makes unsupported transfer mechanically non-authorizing and empirically tests the stability of that authority across project partitions.

The conceptual advance is a knowledge-based permission system around selective prediction: evidence generation, authority qualification, and action execution become separate, auditable layers. This fits the journal's interests in knowledge-based decision support, uncertainty-aware machine learning, and intelligent security systems. The manuscript is original and, at the time of KBS submission, will not be under consideration by another journal or published elsewhere. The authors received no specific funding and declare no competing interests. The manuscript includes data/code availability, CRediT, ethics, and generative-AI disclosure statements.
