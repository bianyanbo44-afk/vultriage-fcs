# Reviewer Audit

This pre-submission audit uses the frozen extension-v2 manuscript, evidence package, validation manifests, and PaperSpine reviewer criteria. It does not predict acceptance or replace external peer review.

## Reviewer Value Map

| Criterion | Current evidence | Residual reviewer risk | Preemptive response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Novelty | VulTriage integrates class-asymmetric weighted sets, a frozen label-free support gate, and an explicit review state in a sealed cross-project vulnerability workflow. | Weighted conformal prediction, selective prediction, and code-model deferral are established components. | Claim system and evaluation novelty, not a new conformal theorem or first-in-field priority; state the nearest-work delta explicitly. |
| Significance | Hashing-SGD improves maximum relative violation on all 24 external projects at the primary point, with a median 0.8161 reduction and a quantified 15.9 percentage-point coverage cost. | The effect does not transfer to the frozen-CodeBERT track, and the base detectors are not SOTA fine-tuned models. | Make detector conditionality the scientific conclusion: the decision layer can work strongly for one detector-shift pair and fail for another. |
| Technical soundness | Configuration, project selection, gate, prediction files, and target-label join order are frozen and hashed; project is the independent unit; exact tests, bootstrap intervals, and Holm adjustment are specified. | Estimated density ratios do not provide oracle guarantees; the gate has PrimeVul LOPO AUROC 0.35; CodeBERT seed addresses reuse one deterministic fit per project. | Keep all three facts in the abstract, Methods, Results, and limitations; avoid target-risk certification and independent-seed language for CodeBERT. |
| Evidence sufficiency | The study uses PrimeVul development, 24 DiverseVul external projects, two detector tracks, 25 method labels, five seed addresses, nine budget pairs, calibration-size sensitivity, near-duplicate sensitivity, and efficiency audits. | There is one external dataset, no fine-tuned transformer/data-flow detector, and no prospective analyst study. | Frame the paper as a decision-layer generalization test, release the complete summary/validation snapshot, and state the required future replications. |
| Baseline fairness | Forced prediction, matched MSP/entropy/temperature baselines, pooled and Mondrian conformal sets, a deterministic PROM-derived four-expert adapter, weighting clips, and gate ablations are retained. | PROM is adapted to binary deterministic evaluation and is not an official reproduction; no LineVul or DeepDFA experiment is run. | Label PROM precisely, cite its provenance and bug fix, and avoid using related detector papers as experimental comparators that were not executed. |
| Clarity | Fig. 1 defines the deploy-or-review interface; Results separate gate discrimination, raw weighting, supported-project coverage, mechanical attainment, sensitivity, and efficiency. | Readers may confuse support pass, singleton coverage, empirical attainment, and formal risk control. | Define each term before use and repeat the distinction where review-only zeros could mislead. |
| Reproducibility | Independent artifact and evidence validators pass; figure source data, summary tables, frozen configuration, hashes, and a privacy-bounded snapshot builder are prepared. | Raw datasets and large private intermediates are intentionally excluded, so reproduction requires users to acquire public data and rerun compute-heavy stages. | State the exclusion boundary and provide deterministic build/validation instructions rather than implying one-click reproduction from summaries alone. |
| Venue fit | The manuscript combines vulnerability detection, distribution shift, statistical decision making, selective automation, and reproducible software experimentation. | An editor may see a generic conformal-prediction paper or a weak-detector benchmark. | Keep the vulnerability triage decision, project shift, external dataset, review workload, and detector-conditional deployment implication in the title, abstract, introduction, and conclusion. |

## Reviewer Objection Register

| Likely objection | Severity | Preemptive fix or current disposition |
|---|---|---|
| The headline is positive only for hashing-SGD. | High | The title says detector-conditional; the abstract reports the CodeBERT null result; Discussion treats the contrast as the main generalization boundary. |
| The support gate is not credible because PrimeVul LOPO AUROC is 0.35. | High | Retain the negative development result, report the external hashing association separately, and describe the gate as an empirical diagnostic rather than a certificate. |
| Support qualification is being mistaken for target-risk control. | High | Report supported-project attainment (2/13 hashing, 0/22 CodeBERT), identify review-only mechanical zeros, and prohibit guarantee language throughout. |
| The weighting effect could simply trade automation for lower violation. | High | Report the 0.1587 singleton-coverage loss, all 24 directional losses, review load, and PROM coverage controls beside the risk result. |
| CodeBERT uses copied seed addresses rather than five independent fits. | High | State one deterministic liblinear fit per project in Methods, Results interpretation, evidence bank, and efficiency accounting. |
| The detector baselines are not modern fine-tuned SOTA systems. | High | Position CodeBERT as a frozen semantic representation and the study as post-hoc decision-layer evidence; retain this as an external-validity limitation. |
| Cross-dataset duplicates may inflate transfer. | High | Apply exact cross-dataset exclusion and report the Jaccard >= 0.90 sensitivity excluding 15,026 target rows; do not claim all semantic duplicates are removed. |
| The 24/24 result is selected from many budgets or methods. | Medium | Identify `(0.10,0.20)` as frozen from v1, retain all nine cells and unfavorable detector outcomes, and use the preregistered comparator family with Holm correction. |
| PROM comparison may be misrepresented. | Medium | Use `PROM-derived (binary, deterministic bug-fixed adapter)` and never call it official PROM or a reproduction of the original evaluation. |
| Runtime numbers imply a speedup despite different hardware execution. | Medium | Call the efficiency audit observational and prohibit speedup, energy, or architecture-efficiency claims. |
| The public repository could leak labels, raw code, or predictions. | High | Run the snapshot deny-list audit and remote-content verification before submission; publish only code, metadata, summaries, manifests, figures, and manuscript sources. |
| Co-corresponding author details or CRediT roles lack final approval. | Submission blocker | Keep the manuscript statement clean, but require both authors to confirm contact details, authorship, and roles in the portal before dispatch. |

## Editorial Fit Map

The strongest Frontiers of Computer Science framing is a software-security deployment study rather than a generic conformal-method paper. The editor-facing contribution is a reproducible decision layer that quantifies when cross-project predictions can be automated, how much review load is introduced, and why the conclusion changes across detector tracks. The principal scientific risks are the negative PrimeVul gate development result, the lack of a positive CodeBERT transfer, and the absence of a fine-tuned modern detector. Those risks are visible and bounded rather than concealed. The remaining non-scientific submission blockers are final coauthor approval and successful remote verification of the public reproducibility snapshot.
