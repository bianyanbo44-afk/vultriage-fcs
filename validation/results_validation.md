| 5.1 Base detectors are comparable, not interchangeable | C2/C5: the study includes a low-cost lexical detector and a frozen semantic representation without treating either as a SOTA detector. | Across 24 projects, median PR-AUC is 0.188 for hashing-SGD and 0.193 for CodeBERT; PR-AUC, F1, and MCC bootstrap intervals overlap. CodeBERT has higher error-detection AUROC but worse Brier score. | Table 2 | Five seed addresses averaged within target project; 10,000-project bootstrap; CodeBERT deterministic seed-copy policy disclosed. | The two tracks provide a meaningful detector contrast for the decision layer. | Do not claim detector superiority, independent five-seed CodeBERT variation, or SOTA vulnerability detection. |
| 5.2 Support is detector-conditional | C6: the frozen label-free gate must be evaluated separately by domain and detector. | PrimeVul LOPO AUROC is 0.35. DiverseVul AUROC is 0.8601 for hashing-SGD, with pass-minus-fail median -0.5473 and interval `[-1.0057,-0.2244]`; CodeBERT AUROC is 0.4286 and its interval crosses zero. | Fig. 2 | Gate frozen from 12 PrimeVul pseudo-target projects before any DiverseVul model output; 24 external project units at the primary budget. | External support discrimination is present for the hashing detector in this cohort and absent for CodeBERT. | Do not claim a detector-general support certificate, causal gate effect, or target-risk guarantee. |
| 5.3 Weighting improves risk alignment for hashing-SGD | C3/C4/C5: estimated weighting should improve class-asymmetric alignment only where the detector-shift pair supports it, and its automation cost must be reported. | Hashing maximum-relative-violation difference is -0.8161 with interval `[-1.7219,-0.3174]`, 24 wins, and adjusted `p=5.96e-7`; singleton coverage falls by 0.1587 in all 24 projects. CodeBERT difference is -0.0052 with interval crossing zero and 12 wins/12 losses. | Fig. 4 and paired-comparison table | Raw clip-20 estimated weighting versus unweighted Mondrian at `(0.10,0.20)`; project-level paired inference after seed averaging; Holm adjustment. | Weighting yields a positive, statistically supported project-level risk-alignment result for hashing-SGD with a quantified review cost. | Do not pool detectors, claim free utility improvement, or infer oracle weighting or arbitrary-shift validity. |
| 5.4 Automation, support, and attainment remain distinct | C7: the deploy-or-review interface must not reward total refusal. | The full gate supports 13/24 hashing and 22/24 CodeBERT projects. Supported-project median singleton coverage is 0.509 and 0.749, while both-budget attainment is only 2/13 and 0/22. Overall full-gate mechanical attainment includes review-only failures. | Fig. 3 and primary-method summary | Gate-fail projects return only the doubleton review set; supported and unsupported denominators are reported separately. | The method exposes how much automation survives support filtering and how often supported decisions meet observed budgets. | Do not count zero-coverage gate failures as predictive successes or describe singleton coverage as accuracy. |
| 5.5 Sensitivity and resource observations | C8 and the resource boundary: the main detector contrast should persist under calibration and duplication stress, while runtime remains observational. | Hashing weighted violation stays below unweighted at all four calibration fractions and changes from 0.483 to 0.434 after near-duplicate exclusion; CodeBERT shows no consistent gain.
