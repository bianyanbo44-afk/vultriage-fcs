This is a new, author-authorized retrospective analysis of existing released benchmark labels and sealed predictions. Original study and neural v3.1 outcomes are known. The choices below are fixed before these new comparisons are computed; this is neither an outcome-blind preregistration nor evidence of prospective deployment. The machine-readable configuration is `configs/capacity_feedback_v4.json`. Its SHA-256, this protocol, implementation and test bytes are bound in a freeze receipt before outcome execution. No earlier config, prediction, seal, result, reviewer packet or manuscript is modified.

## Questions, data and units

1. With a hard integer analyst-review capacity, which policies complete the full incoming function roster, what automatic-label allocation do they produce, and what illustrative cost results under explicit miss/review assumptions?
2. In ordered batches, does updating only the probability calibrator/policy from delayed labels of previously reviewed functions change a static source-calibrated policy? How much reviewed feedback actually arrives, and how much unresolved review remains?

All six DiverseVul unsupported-domain projects (bdwgc, evolution-ews, jerryscript, psutil, stunnel, unbound) retain their sealed review-all fallback, including every affected seed.

The reporting unit is the project after averaging genuine seeds. Functions and seeds are not independent deployments. ## Capacity design

Fix project capacities to floor(rN), where r is 0, .05, .10, .20, .40, .60 or 1 and N is the full project roster. A reviewed function consumes one slot. This is an explicit analyst-capacity scenario, not a measurement of analyst time or staffing. The primary capacity comparison uses r=.20, miss cost10, false-alert cost1, review cost.1 and correct review.

At alpha_v=.10, alpha_s=.20 and density-ratio clip20, the original weighted TRACE singleton actions are preserved. Empty and doubleton sets still require review. If the required review count exceeds capacity, serve only the cap and retain excess items as unresolved backlog. Do not force unsupported automation, drop rows, count deferred items as successfully reviewed, or claim this policy completes the roster. Review priority is decreasing source-calibrated expected automatic-error cost minus expected review cost, with stable original target-position ties. Mandatory review is mandatory even where this saving is not positive. All-review has the same hard-cap/backlog accounting.

Report seven fixed policies: TRACE mandatory review; disjoint probability tails matched to TRACE's two automatic-label counts; global confidence matched to TRACE's automation count; source-isotonic expected-cost selection with a hard cap; confidence-based review at the cap with argmax automation elsewhere; forced argmax; and mandatory all-review. The two matched policies preserve the original weighted automatic volume/allocation and therefore may also be infeasible. The cap-based source-isotonic policy chooses the cheaper automatic action, breaking equal expected action cost toward vulnerable, then reviews strictly positive expected cost savings up to capacity. High capacity must not compel economically needless review. MSP reviews the least-confident cap rows and uses safe argmax at exact p=.5. Ties use original sealed target position.

For every policy export auto-safe, auto-vulnerable, reviewed, unresolved, completed and required-review counts; actual hard capacity; infeasibility; automatic misses/false alerts and their original class denominators. Ordinary complete-roster cost is undefined where unresolved work remains. Also show a clearly named lower-bound cost that omits unresolved harm and a deadline sensitivity that charges each unresolved vulnerable function the miss cost. The latter is an assumed consequence of unresolved work at the observation deadline, not an observed automatic miss. This prevents free backlog from appearing as a favorable full-roster policy.

Cost scenarios cross miss10/50 with review.01/.1/1, falsealert1 and perfect review. Add miss10/50, review.1 with expected review false-negative/false-positive probability .05. These latter costs are analytical expected-error scenarios, not sampled or measured human errors. In every scenario, source-isotonic review selection includes the expected review error cost. Report all scenarios and label all units illustrative; none estimates real team monetary cost.

## Ordering and feedback design

Prepared MegaVul metadata contains complete commit-author epoch timestamps and ISO dates. Use recorded commit-author order, grouping full commit identities and all equal-date commits into one block before batching. Neither these dates nor published vulnerability-fix records establish when analysts first observed code, when a label became known, or when a model ran. PrimeVul raw and prepared source rows lack dates. No source-before-target chronological holdout is claimed. DiverseVul raw and prepared target rows lack dates, so use a deterministic salted hash order of full commit identities and explicitly label every result synthetic-order simulation.

Build consecutive batches greedily toward 128 functions without splitting a commit/date block. If an atomic block alone exceeds 128, keep it whole and record the oversized batch. Equal dates and all functions of one commit must never be split across batches. Invalid/missing/inconsistent MegaVul dates stop that project's temporal execution and are reported; they must not be silently reinterpreted as real chronology. Export batch order, dates, full row-position membership, commit counts and actual batch sizes before label access.

Fix integer review capacity to 8, 16 or 32 slots per batch, independent of batch size, and label delays to 1, 3 or 5 batches. Primary feedback setting is 16 slots, delay3, miss10, falsealert1, review.1 and correct review. No additional free drain occurs after the final batch. A previously reviewed function's benchmark label arrives at the start of service_batch+delay. Feedback therefore uses reviewed labels only; unreviewed automatic items and unresolved queue items supply no calibration labels. Each reviewed item is already priced and charged to its service batch's cap. There is no full-label feedback arm.

Compare six methods: static and rolling source-isotonic cost policies; static and rolling TRACE mandatory-review queues; static MSP; static forced argmax. All see the same incoming ordered roster and per-batch slot cap. TRACE pools older unresolved mandatory-review items with current mandatory-review items, serves at most the fixed slots using calibrated expected-risk priority, and preserves singleton membership. Source cost policies make a current-batch automatic decision whenever they do not economically review; they need no unresolved queue. The rolling TRACE variant changes queue priority only, not the sealed set membership, detector, or density ratios.

Initial probabilities are the sealed isotonic source-calibration map. At the beginning of a batch, an eligible rolling update fits increasing isotonic regression on the original source calibration and the most recent 512 arrived reviewed target rows. Source rows together have total anchor weight100, arrived target rows weight1 each. Eligibility requires at least30 arrived target labels and at least5 of each class in the window. Before eligibility retain the source initializer. Once fitted, retain the last eligible fit if a later window is ineligible. Refit only when new labels arrive. Target feedback is project-specific and never pooled across project/dataset/seed runs. These fixed heuristic choices are not tuned from new target outcomes.

Review-selected labels are biased toward the chosen queue. They need not represent automatic/unreviewed functions. Rolling isotonic calibration has no claimed target calibration guarantee, and no new conformal guarantee is attached. Inherited sealed TRACE density-ratio fits use the entire unlabeled target project; this is transductive benchmark input, not a newly demonstrated online domain-fitting pipeline. Primary source-isotonic simulations need source calibration, current frozen detector scores and past arrived reviewed labels only.

Fit receipts and batch ledgers record reviewed/service/arrival identities, available feedback, window class counts, fit status, calibration knot hashes and the greatest service/arrival batch of any target label used. Assertions require service_batch < current_batch and arrival_batch <= current_batch for all used labels, reviewed membership for every released label, unique service of each function, and served_count <= fixed cap in every batch. The label oracle cannot reveal labels from current/future service batches or unreviewed items. Per-function probability calibration is measured using the map available when the function first enters its batch, before any label from that batch.
